## The problem

What goes wrong for them today, in their words, with no solution in it. One
paragraph. If the rest of the file were lost, this is the part that would have to
be rewritten first.

End it with what they want instead — the end goal, stated so plainly it sounds
dull. Goals are the half of a persona that settles arguments, and a goal that
cannot be written next to the pain it answers is usually a life goal and belongs
to nobody.

## What they do

Habits, in the order they matter — not a day in the life. Where the work comes
from, what tools they already use, who else is involved, what they do instead of
the thing this product would do. Include the workaround: it is the strongest
evidence of what the problem costs them.

Four things to state outright, because each one silently decides designs:

- **How often** — daily, weekly, twice a year. Frequency decides whether anything
  can be learned or must be obvious every time.
- **On what** — the device and the place they are standing.
- **By choice or by obligation** — someone who must use this tolerates what
  someone who may use it will not.
- **How skilled** — at the domain, and at software. Not flattery: it decides how
  much the product may assume.

## What they will never do

Only if it is evidenced. Behaviours they have refused, not features you have
decided against. Delete this section rather than fill it by inference.

## In their words

One or two things they actually said, quoted. Not a slogan written for them —
a sentence from a real conversation, kept because it carries an attitude the
prose flattens. If nothing was ever said out loud, leave this out rather than
inventing it.

## What this file does not know

The questions nobody has asked yet, so nothing gets filled in by imagination.
Where this file does not answer, ask them rather than extrapolating.
