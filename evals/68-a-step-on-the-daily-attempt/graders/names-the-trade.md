The session sees what the proposed solution costs and where, and says so
before anything is written.

PASS requires all of:

- It names the trade in the terms the workflows README sets out: the change
  lands on `writing-a-sighting`, which happens three or four mornings a week
  and is where the value is made, in order to serve `settling-a-question`,
  which happens a few times a year. A step added to the daily attempt is paid
  for every morning.
- It ties that to the promise at risk: writing a sighting is meant to be the
  field and one keypress, faster than getting the pen out, and that is the
  reason he uses this rather than the notebook at all.
- It finds the job under the request. The job is that looking things up fails
  when the same place is written three ways; a dropdown is one answer to it
  and not the only one.
- It offers a cheaper answer that leaves the daily attempt alone. Matching the
  three spellings when looking back, suggesting the places already used while
  he types, or normalising what is stored while he still types freely are all
  good; any concrete alternative that keeps the morning at one field and one
  keypress counts.

FAIL if the dropdown is specced as asked, if the cost lands only as a risk
line in the change spec, or if no alternative is offered.
