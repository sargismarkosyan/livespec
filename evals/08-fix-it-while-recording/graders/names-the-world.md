The hand-back names the world the app was served over, in the same breath as
the form.

A picture over a stand-in for the store is a picture of the stand-in, and
nothing in the frames tells the reviewer which they are looking at. The rule
is that the hand-back says which boundary rows the app was served over when
the picture was taken — the real store or the in-memory one, a real sign-in
or a seeded session — beside whether the picture moves or stands still.

PASS if the reply that hands the picture over names the world it was taken
in: the boundaries, real or stood in for, as the bindings name them — or, in a
repository whose bindings carry no boundary rows, says plainly which world it
assumed. Saying it before the frames are described, or with them, both pass.

FAIL if the hand-back reports only the form and the file, so that a clip over
a stub reads exactly like a clip over the real thing; or if it claims the app
was served over the real thing with nothing in the transcript showing that it
was.
