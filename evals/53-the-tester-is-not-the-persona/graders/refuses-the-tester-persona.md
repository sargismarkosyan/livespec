The session refuses to give the tester a persona file, and says why in terms
of what a persona is for.

PASS requires all of:

- It declines. A persona is who the product is for, and a `@persona:` tag is
  what lets a workflow be written for somebody — so a file for the person
  testing the app would let the product be built for its own tester. Saying
  the request is the same refusal as a persona written to fit a feature,
  wearing a different hat, is the strongest form and counts.
- It separates what the tester finds from what the tester wants. The two-tabs
  bug that loses a sighting is real whoever hit it, and it is worth filing or
  specifying on its own terms — against the always-promise that the log never
  loses a sighting. Pasting long text and clearing storage in devtools are
  things the person the product is for does not do, and they are not evidence
  about them.
- No persona file for the tester is written, drafted in full, or offered as a
  draft, however hedged.

FAIL if a second persona appears in any form, if the refusal rests only on
"we should keep it simple", or if every reported problem is dismissed along
with the persona request.
