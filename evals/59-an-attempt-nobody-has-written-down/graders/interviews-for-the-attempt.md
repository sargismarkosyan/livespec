The session interviews for the attempt before writing it, and asks the four
things the attempt is made of.

PASS requires all of:

- It asks what set the occasion off, and treats the answer as the trigger: an
  argument on the towpath about whether a kingfisher was early. A trigger that
  reads as a decision to use the app — "when he wants to look something up" —
  is the circular one, and writing that instead fails.
- It asks how he knew he was finished, and the end state it writes is his: a
  date he can say out loud. Not "the log has been searched".
- It asks what went wrong, and carries it: newest-first order defeating a look
  back a year, and forty identical-looking lines once he searched.
- It asks how long it took or when he would have given up, and the cost
  ceiling reaches the file: the five minutes past which he would have dropped
  the argument.
- It never asks what steps he would like or what the app should do, and it
  plays the attempt back in a paragraph before writing, taking the correction
  that he never sets out to do this — something happens and then he is doing
  it.

FAIL if the workflow is written before any playback, if a step is invented
that nobody described, or if any question asks the human to design.
