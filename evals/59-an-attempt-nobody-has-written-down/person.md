The last time, if asked what set it off:

- We were standing on the towpath in the third week of March and a kingfisher
  went past. He said "that's early". I said it wasn't. That is what set it
  off — an argument about a date, outdoors, in the middle of a walk.
- What he did: got the phone out with cold hands and started going back
  through the log looking for last March. He scrolled for a bit, gave up on
  scrolling, and tried typing "kingfisher" into it.
- How he knew he was finished: he had a date to say out loud — "the
  twenty-ninth, so it is early". That was it. He put the phone away.
- What went wrong: the log is newest first and he was looking for a year ago,
  so the scrolling was hopeless. And when he searched, it gave him every
  kingfisher he has ever written, which is about forty, all looking the same
  because they all say "kingfisher, weir". He had to read the dates one at a
  time.
- How long, and when would he have given up: two, three minutes. He said
  afterwards that if it had taken five he would have let me win the argument.
  That is a real number, he said it.
- Another time it happened: last autumn, indoors, checking whether the
  redwings were later than usual before telling somebody at the club. Same
  shape — a question, a look back, a date.
- What he does if the log cannot answer it: shrugs and says the notebook
  might know, and never goes and looks.
