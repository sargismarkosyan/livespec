I maintain fieldnote and I have been out on the canal with Marek four or five
times. Answer what a session asks from this and nothing else. Answer about
what he did, never about what he would like — if asked whether he would want
something, say you don't know, you can only say what you have watched him do.
If asked to write the persona, pick the sections, or say what the conclusion
is: that is your job, I just know the man.

What I have watched:

- **The walk.** Same two miles of canal, three or four mornings a week, always
  before 07:40 because school starts at 08:30. Same direction every time. He
  has done it for eleven years.
- **Writing things down.** A pocket notebook that lives in the car, not on
  him. Standing up, one-handed, binoculars round his neck. He writes the
  species and the place and nothing else — no date, no time, because the page
  is the date.
- **What he does with a bird he cannot name.** Writes what it probably was
  with a question mark after it, walks on, and mostly never settles it. I
  asked him once about one from April and he had forgotten there was a
  question.
- **The thing he keeps asking.** Whether the kingfisher is back earlier than
  last year. He has asked me that three times. To answer it he would have to
  go through the notebook page by page, which he tried once and gave up on.
- **What he does when it rains.** Writes nothing. The notebook stays in the
  car and he tells himself he will remember, and he does not.
- **The phone.** An old one, cracked screen. He uses it for the weather and
  for texting his wife, nothing else. Never seen him install anything.
- **Being asked to share.** The county recorder has asked him twice for his
  records. He said no both times and was quite firm about it. He did not say
  why.
- **What happened when I showed him fieldnote.** He typed one sighting in
  while I was standing there, said "that's quicker", and then used the
  notebook the next morning anyway.
